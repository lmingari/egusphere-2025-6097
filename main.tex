\begin{abstract}
%
% 1. Motivation
%
Ensemble-based modelling of the atmospheric dispersal of volcanic clouds enables more realistic forecasts by explicitly accounting for uncertainties in eruption source parameters, meteorological data, and systematic errors in transport models.
%
% 2. Problem
%
Many ensemble applications, including forecast uncertainty quantification, data assimilation, and probabilistic hazard assessment, require large ensembles to reduce sampling errors and adequately represent probability distributions. 
%
However, simulating and processing large ensembles with atmospheric dispersal models can be computationally demanding, even on high-performance computing clusters. Generative AI offers a complementary approach by representing simulated fields in a lower-dimensional latent space, enabling new samples to be generated at low computational cost and facilitating the manipulation of large ensembles.
%
% 3. Method
%
A Variational AutoEncoder (VAE) is trained on ensembles of physics-based simulations and used to generate new samples. The learned latent-space representation is then used to test two ensemble-based data assimilation methods, demonstrating the potential benefits of performing data assimilation in a lower-dimensional space.
%
% 4. Proof-of-concept scope
%
As a proof of concept, the data assimilation problem is formulated in two dimensions.
%
% 5. Results
%
Two numerical experiments demonstrate that VAE-generated ensembles can reproduce key statistics of a larger reference ensemble and improve agreement with satellite retrievals. The VAE achieves up to a 1000-fold compression of the original fields into 16- and 32-dimensional latent spaces. The VAE-generated ensemble of 4096 members reduces RMSE by 4\% relative to the model prior, while latent-space data assimilation further reduces RMSE by 30\%.
%
% 6. Implications
%
These results highlight the potential of generative AI to efficiently augment physics-based ensembles and improve forecasting skill through latent-space data assimilation.
\end{abstract}
